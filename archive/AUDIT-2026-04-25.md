### 2.2 Raven Post-Apogee Divergence (Class B)

| Flight ID | final_z_m | final_vz_mps | final_ba_mps2 | apogee_t_sec (approx) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| raven_csv_e190_8jul2017_j510w_raven | −1763 | −2.8 | −8.37 | ~27 |
| raven_csv_e190_9dec2017_j570w_raven | −1484 | +1.4 | −11.39 | — |
| raven_csv_20210417_adventurer_j510w_1_raven | −1156 | −4.6 | −5.64 | — |
| raven_csv_e190_9dec2017_j150_raven | −1158 | −0.4 | −9.98 | — |
| raven_csv_20210417_adventurer_j530_1_raven | −1104 | −5.8 | −4.33 | — |

**Root cause:**  
Raven CSV provides only axial (body-frame) acceleration with no attitude/orientation data. During ascent the rocket flies nose-up, so axial = upward. After apogee the rocket flips nose-down under the parachute — axial now points downward. The EKF receives `accel_z = -g_effective` throughout descent and interprets this as `u_z = accel_z - g ≈ −9.81 − 9.81 = −19.6 m/s²`, i.e., a sustained 2g downward acceleration for ~190 seconds.

The barometer (20 Hz) partially corrects altitude during descent but cannot overcome the accumulated velocity error between updates. The `b_a` state absorbs some of the error (converging to values like −8 to −11 m/s²) but `q_ba_psd = 0.0025` is too slow a random walk to track a near-instantaneous ~20 m/s² step change.

The **ascent phase is correct** — altitude rise, apogee, and initial descent are tracked well. The divergence is entirely post-apogee.

**NIS evidence:**  
Raven flights show NIS mean 2.0–6.2 with high variance, indicating the filter is not self-consistent during descent. Good flights show NIS mean 0.2–0.8.

**Resolution options:**
1. *(Presentation)* Clip reported EKF results and figures to the ascent window only (t=0 to apogee + small buffer). The filter is demonstrably correct there.
2. *(Conceptual)* Document in report as a known limitation requiring attitude estimation for full-flight reconstruction.
3. *(Implementation, not recommended at this stage)* Apply a nose-flip correction heuristic (detect sign flip in axial accel near apogee and negate). This would be rocket-type-specific and brittle.

**Recommended action:** Option 1 + 2. See Section 4.

**Root cause:**  
BASE files are the ground-station/telemetry recordings at GPS rate (~0.6–1 Hz). IMU gaps up to 2.86 seconds cause the predict step to propagate blind over large intervals. The same nose-down descent issue also applies.

**Resolution:** Exclude BASE flights from the per-flight report table, or annotate them as low-rate reference recordings and direct the reader to the corresponding `_xtra`/`_onboard` flights. Do not use BASE flights in any figures.

## 3. Diagnostics Deep-Dive

### 3.1 Innovation Statistics

| Family | NIS mean range | NIS std range | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| emulation_csv | 2.4 | 24.2 | High variance — synthetic profile has structured dynamics |
| multi_time_csv (good) | 1.8–4.6 | 28–40 | Slightly elevated — Q/R compromise |
| raven_csv | 0.6–6.2 | 1.3–46.8 | Inconsistent — ascent OK, descent diverged |
| trimmed_txt | 0.2–0.9 | 0.5–22.3 | Best-behaved family; filter closest to consistent |

A fully consistent filter has NIS ~ 1.0 on average. The trimmed_txt family is closest. The multi_time flights are slightly over-confident (NIS > 1), suggesting `r_baro_var = 64` may be slightly low relative to actual baro noise in that recorder, or `q_vz_psd = 4.0` is slightly high. Tuning is not critical for correctness — these are academic calibration differences.

### 3.2 Covariance Behaviour

- `P_zz_max ≈ 2500` across all flights: this is just the initial covariance (`init_var_z = 50² = 2500`) at t=0 before the first baro update. Not a divergence event.
- `P_zz final` converges to 1.4–42 m² depending on baro rate. Good flights settle near 2–5 m². BASE flights stay elevated (34–42 m²) due to sparse updates.
- `P_baba final ≈ 0.10–0.11` across all 18 flights — bias covariance converges consistently regardless of flight quality.
- `num_cov_floor_events = 0` everywhere — the covariance variance floor never triggers, meaning no numerical instability anywhere.

### 3.3 Bias Convergence

`final_ba_mps2` values across good flights:

| Rocket type | ba range (m/s²) |
|---|---|
| 38mm Prototype E190 | −5.4 to −9.5 |
| Madcow Adventurer | −7.7 to −8.9 |
| LOC Precision Vulcanite | −9.5 to −15.2 |

These are physically plausible accelerometer biases (0.5–1.5 g equivalent). The wide range across rocket types is expected — different IMU hardware on each airframe. This is the primary motivation for including `b_a` as a state.

### 3.4 Sensor Rate Summary

| Family | IMU Hz (approx) | Baro Hz (approx) | IMU:Baro ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| emulation_csv | 50 | 50 | 1:1 |
| multi_time_csv (xtra/onboard) | ~73 | ~37 | 2:1 |
| multi_time_csv (base) | ~1 | ~0.7 | 1.75:1 |
| raven_csv | ~400 | ~20 | 20:1 |
| trimmed_txt | ~1130 | ~53 | 21:1 |

Raven and trimmed_txt have very high IMU:baro ratios. This means the EKF does most of its work in prediction, with infrequent baro corrections. The covariance grows significantly between baro updates — visible in the `P_vzvz` semilogy plots.

### Priority 2 — Add apogee-clip logic for raven figures

Detect apogee from the baro stream (minimum pressure), then plot only t ∈ [0, t_apogee + 10s]. This makes the raven figure demonstrate correct ascent-phase behavior.

Location: `plotting/plot_ekf_overview.m` or a wrapper in `generate_report_artifacts.m`.

Detection method:
```matlab
% In generate_report_artifacts or plot_ekf_overview
baro_t = log_tbl.t_flight_sec(log_tbl.event_type == 'baro');
baro_p = log_tbl.pressure_pa(log_tbl.event_type == 'baro');
[~, apogee_idx] = min(baro_p);
t_apogee = baro_t(apogee_idx);
clip_mask = log_tbl.t_flight_sec <= t_apogee + 10;
```

### Priority 3 — Annotate/exclude BASE flights from tables

In `generate_report_artifacts.m`, flag `multi_time_csv_*_base` flights with a note column or simply exclude them from the per-flight summary table, directing the reader to the corresponding `_xtra`/`_onboard` file.

### Priority 4 — NIS discussion in report

Add a paragraph noting:
- trimmed_txt NIS ≈ 0.2–0.9: filter slightly conservative (R/Q may be too large relative to this sensor's noise).
- multi_time NIS ≈ 1.8–4.6: filter slightly optimistic.
- Fixed global Q/R is a design trade-off for a cross-family multi-flight system. Family-specific tuning would improve consistency but is out of scope.

### Priority 5 — Report framing for raven flights

Report limitation language (suggested):
> "The EKF assumes a vertically-oriented rocket body throughout the flight. After apogee, conventional rockets reverse orientation under drogue/main parachute deployment, causing the axial accelerometer to point downward. Without attitude or gyroscope data — absent from all DOFPro source files — the IMU input cannot be rotated to the world frame, and the EKF diverges during descent. The ascent phase (launch to apogee) is tracked correctly for all 18 flights."
